WORLDVIEW ── The view of history that led to SHINDO

We have handed
the foundations of our lives
to someone far away

Someone else grows our food, energy arrives from far away, a home is something you buy, and learning happens at school. The ways people once supported one another have been replaced by services paid for with money. Over the past eighty years life has become astonishingly convenient — and at the same time we have lost the ability to support the foundations of our own lives.

The assumptions
behind that convenience
are shifting dramatically

That cheap energy will be available. That the world will stay at peace and supply chains will keep moving. That money will hold its value and food will keep being imported. Yet abrupt climate shifts, conflicts over resources, structural change driven by AI, farming that is losing its farmers, and wavering trust in currencies — every one of those assumptions is being shaken.

A NEW BEGINNING ── A time to begin again

Creating a village-scale foundation to carry us through a great transition

This is not the beginning of the end; it is a time to create together, in resonance with one another. Not to build out of fear, but out of the wish to live better. That difference will profoundly shape the world that is created from here.

What SHINDO creates are living assets: soil that bears fruit every year, water that keeps flowing, houses that can be lived in for generations, the wisdom to make it through the world, and companions to create and live with.

WHAT WE ARE BUILDING ── What we are trying to create

Five pillars, built in Oku-Aizu.

What we are trying to build is a new operating system for everyday life, one that can keep life going in the era ahead. Rather than leaning on outside dependence, we will raise five foundations that we can maintain and renew ourselves, step by step toward 2030. They are not separate businesses; they grow together, linked within a single village.
