BASE ── Community base

Community base: Rikū

Aizumisato, Fukushima. SHINDO's first base, completed in January 2025 after a two-year restoration of an old farmhouse.

INTRO

Choosing to inherit, not demolish.

Rikū, SHINDO's first base, stands in a hillside hamlet in Aizumisato, Fukushima. Completed in January 2025 after two years of restoring an old farmhouse, it is our place for creating together in good health. Many meaningful ways of using Rikū are now opening up.

NAME ── Where the name comes from

Exploring the principle of emptiness.

The name Rikū came to Kojima, head of Munokai Natural Farm, in a dream one day. Kū — emptiness — is the complete state in which nothing exists because everything does. Ri is principle. The name carries a prayer: that this be a place where people gather to intuit that principle through every kind of practice.

HISTORY ── What the house carries

A house that has lived through 250 years.

01

An encounter on the verge of demolition

In autumn 2022, the house was already scheduled for demolition. Its thatched roof was to become the main ingredient in Munokai's compost, and the building itself was to be torn down. We asked for the work to be postponed, and took the house on.

02

A house that survived the Aizu peasant uprising

The former village headman's house, standing since the mid-Edo period. It was the first house of the hamlet, founded by the family of Baba Mino-no-kami, a descendant of Takeda Shingen. It came through the Aizu yonaoshi uprising of 1868, and the hatchet marks left by the peasants who stormed it remain in the pillars to this day.

03

Keep the history; demolish nothing

An old farmhouse is the wisdom of living with nature, condensed into architecture. Rather than destroy it, we chose to put it to use and carry it on. Receiving the intentions of the land's ancestors and of the peasants who joined the uprising — that was the concept behind Rikū's restoration.

RESTORATION ── Two years with Aizu's master carpenters

Getting through a reality twice as hard as we expected — just barely.

The work was done by Shiki, a local builder with a long record of healthy houses made from natural materials. Led by a master carpenter who has built more than 700 houses since his mid-teens, it is a team of highly skilled craftsmen rare anywhere in Japan.

Soaring material costs, applying for loans partway through construction — a reality roughly twice as hard as we had expected, overcome one step at a time with the support of our partners. SHINDO's business model was born out of this two-year restoration.

And in January 2025, Rikū was finally complete.

ARCHITECTURE ── What makes the building special

A natural-material house where energy flows well.

The finest natural materials and craftsmanship

Built around naturally dried timber, with reclaimed wood, bamboo, plaster, earth, ancient marine clay, charcoal, copper, and natural stone. Japan's finest natural materials bring the energy of the interior into balance.

A structure where air and energy circulate naturally

Thanks to breathable natural materials and the careful placement of vents, air moves naturally through the house. Breathing comes easier, sleep deepens, and bodily awareness recovers.

Marine-clay walls that radiate far-infrared warmth

The walls are earthen, mixed with natural ancient marine clay. Beyond regulating humidity, they steadily emit far-infrared radiation. The clay-blended walls make the house comfortable without air conditioning in summer and feel warmer than the air temperature in winter — a welcome quality in Aizu's humid summers.

MEANING ── What the place is for

A house that awakens the cells and the mind.

Rikū was never meant to be luxurious or merely comfortable. It was built as a space where sensitivity, judgment, and an open mind grow of their own accord. In a natural environment of strong contrasts, body and mind quietly come alive, and good work and good relationships take root.

This is where experiments in living are brought together, relationships deepen, and the next creation begins — SHINDO's place of restoration and creation, the main house from which the circle of co-creation spreads.

PROOF ── Why this place is evidence that we deliver

Anyone can write a plan. But taking on a 250-year-old farmhouse slated for demolition, overcoming the wall of funding, and rebuilding it over two years with a team of master craftsmen — that experience lives on in how we build our projects today. That Rikū is complete and in use is our solid track record.

ORIGIN ── Where everything began

One farmhouse restoration shaped our vision and our business.

Rikū is SHINDO's base and also its starting point. From the moment we decided to take on a house about to be demolished, we tested our criteria in practice: inherit rather than destroy, choose what accords with the principles of nature, make the people who live there healthier. It was during these two years that scattered ideas came together as the Showa Village vision, and that our current business model — construction, farming, energy, and shared learning working as one — was born.

In other words, Rikū is a working sample of the concept. What happened here, we will try next at the scale of a whole hamlet. That is the vision for Onogawa, Showa Village.
